In the presence of synthetically optimised linear DNA-conjugated F8BTaz SPNs the second-order kinetics typically fitted to TMSD reactions in closed systems no longer apply. Instead, sigmoidal quenching and excitation kinetics exist for an acceptor and donor species, where the kinetic barrier for an invading strand to displace an incumbent strand on the surface of the particle induces a lag phase that must be accounted for.

To this end a logistic fitting function can be applied to both the quenched acceptor and excited donor species. A Five-Parameter Logistic (5PL) fit was chosen over a Four-Parameter Logistic (4PL) as this accounts for asymmetry around the inflection point. The following equation was adapted from Gottschalk P. and Dunn J. (1) for 5PL fitting.

Parameters in equation defined *d* and *a* such that *lim_<sub>{x -> ∞}</sub> y(x)=d* and *lim_<sub>{x -> 0}</sub> y(x)=a*. The parameter *c* represented the inflection point of the 5PL; *p* the slope at *c*, the sign of which determined whether the sigmoidal curve was increasing *(p >0)* or decreasing *(p<0)*, and *s* the difference in rate *p* before and after the inflection point *c*. In a 4PL fit *s =1*. Although 5PL curves fit most sigmoidal relationships, in the context of TMSD reactions on particle surfaces this fit was explicitly chosen based on similar behaviours observed by Lang B. (2) and Ghotra G. et al. (3).
